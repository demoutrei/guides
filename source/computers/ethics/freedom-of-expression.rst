Freedom of Expression
=====================


Why the Internet Changes the Issue
++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

Messages can reach thousands or millions of people quickly and at low cost. Anonymity can protest activists, journalists, whistle-blowers, and minority voices. The same tools can enable defamation, harassment, fraud, illegal content, and propaganda.

**Central question**: *How can technology protect legitimate expression without enabling harm?*


Tor and Online Anonymity
++++++++++++++++++++++++

Tor routes communications through a distributed network to hide a user's location and browsing activity. It can support privacy, safety, human rights, and communication in hostile environments. It can also be misused for criminal activity, showing the dual-use nature of technology.

**Ethical tension**: privacy and freedom versus accountability and public safety.


First Amendment Rights
++++++++++++++++++++++

The First Amendment protects freedom of religion, speech, press, peaceful assembly, and petition. Protection applies to verbal, written, visual, symbolic, and anonymous expression. It primarily limits government interference; private companies and schools may set their own rules. Unpopular or offensive viewpoints may still receive constitutional protection.


Speech Not Protected
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Perjury and fraud.

Defamation
  False statements of fact that harm another person.

Obscene speech.

Incitement of panic or crime, fighting words and sedition.


.. admonition:: Important Distinction
    :class: important
    
    Offensive or unpopular speech is not automatically illegal.


Obscene Speech: Miller Test
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Miller v. California established a three-part test.

Would an average person, using contemporary community standards, find that the work appeals to prurient interest? Does it depict or describe sexual conduct in a patently offensive way defined by state law? Does the work, taken as a whole, lack serious literary, artistic, political, or scientific value?


.. admonition:: Internet Challenge

    Content crosses geographic communities with different standards.


Defamation Online
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

**Defamation** is a false statement presented as fact that harms another person.

**Slander** is spoken defamation; **libel** is written or published defamation.

**Truth** is an absolute defense to a defamation claim.

**Opinions** are generally protected, but users should avoid presenting unsupported accusations as facts.


.. admonition:: Example
    :class: hint

    A negative review should distinguish personal experience from an unproven claim of criminal or harmful conduct.


Controlling Access: Major Laws
++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

Communications Decency Act (CDA)
  Attempted to restrict indecent online material; broad restrictions were ruled unconstitutional.


  Section 230 of the CDA
    Generally protects interactive computer services from liability for user-generated content when they are not treated as the content provider.


Child Online Protection Act (COPA)
  Restrictions on material harmful to minors were rules unconstitutional.


Children's Internet Protection Act (CIPA)
  Requires federally funded schools and libraries to use filters and online-safety policies.


Internet Filters
++++++++++++++++

URL filtering blocks known domains or websites.

Keyword filtering blocks pages containing selected terms.

Dynamic-content filtering evaluates content using techniques such as image or object analysis.


.. admonition:: Benefits
    :class: tip

    Reduces exposure to pornography, malware, or other harmful material.


.. admonition:: Risks
    :class: tip

    Overblocking legitimate content about health, politics, sexuality, religion, or civil rights.


Internet Censorship
+++++++++++++++++++

**Censorship** is the control or suppression of publishing or accessing information online.

Pressure can be applied to web hosts, internet service providers, search engines, DNS providers, and users.

Methods include takedown demands, keyword blocking, content modification, surveillance, and connection termination.


.. admonition:: Ethical concern
    
    Protecting people from harm can become political control or suppression of dissent.


Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation
+++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

An **SLAPP** is typically a lawsuit used to intimidate critics on matters of public interest.

Targets may include citizens, community groups, reviewers, journalists, and activists. Even a weak lawsuit can silence speech through legal costs, stress, and delay.

Anti-SLAPP laws can allow early dismissal and shift legal costs to the abusive plaintiff.


Anonymity and Doxing
++++++++++++++++++++

Anonymous expression can protect people from retaliation and support open discussion.

Anonymous remailers remove identifying headers or IP information before forwarding messages.

**Doxing** is collecting and publishing someone's private information without permission.


.. admonition:: Ethical balance

    Protect privacy and dissent while responding to threats, fraud, and harassment.


John Doe Lawsuits
+++++++++++++++++

A plaintiff may sue an unidentified online defendant using a temporary name such as John Doe. A subpoena can then be requested from an ISP or platform to reveal the person's identity.

Supporters argue anonymity should not shield defamation, trade-secret disclosure, or other wrongdoing. Critics warn that subpoenas can expose critics before wrongdoing is proven. Courts should require a legitimate claim, relevance, and safeguards for anonymous speech.


Hate Speech
+++++++++++

In the United States, annoying, critical, demeaning, or offensive speech is generally protected.

Legal action becomes possible when speech becomes a clear threat, intimidation, or targeted malicious harassment. Private platforms may remove content under their terms of service even when government could not prohibit it.

The key ethical task is distinguishing protected expression from credible, targeted harm.


Pornography and Sexting
+++++++++++++++++++++++

Adult material involving consenting adults is debated under free-expression principles; obscene material may be restricted. Organizations must prevent pornography from creating a hostile workplace or exposing unwilling employees.

Reasonable workplace steps include an acceptable-use policy, monitoring consistent with policy, investigation, and discipline.

Sexting can create irreversible privacy, reputational, and legal consequences, especially when minors are involved.


CAN-SPAM Act
++++++++++++

Commercial e-mail must use accurate sender and routing infomration. Subject lines may not mislead recipients. Messages must identify themselves as advertisements and include a valid physical postal address. Recipients must have a working opt-out method, and opt-out requests must be honored within 10 days.


.. admonition:: Ethical Principle

    Communication should respect consent, transparency, and recipient control.


Fake News and Online Journalism
+++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

Online news spreads rapidly through social media and recomendation systems.

Fake news may be fictional, highly opinionated, or satirical content presented in journalistic style. Responsible journalism emphasizes fact-checking, source verification, balance, and correction.

Users should pause before sharing: check the source, date, evidence, author, and independent confirmation.


Workplace Checklist
+++++++++++++++++++

Maintain a written privacy and IT-use policy.

Explain limits on nonbusiness websites and unacceptable content. Prohibit defamation, targeted hate, pornography, and misuse of anonymous remailers in the workplace.

Define monitoring practices and tell employees what to do when they receive threats or explicit content.

Apply rules consistently and protect good-faith reporting from retaliation.


Key Ethical Tensions
++++++++++++++++++++

Freedom versus Safety
  Restricting harmful content may also restrict legitimate speech.


Anonymity versus Accountability
  Anonymity protects vulnerable speakers but can hide abuse.


Platform responsibility versus Neutrality
  Moderation can reduce harm but shape public debate.


Privacy versus Investigation
  Identifying a speaker may be justified only with strong evidence and due process.


Speed versus Truth
  Viral content rewards immediacy, while accuracy requires verification.


Practical Decisions Framework
+++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

1. Identify the exact expression and the people affected.

2. Separate fact, opinion, satire, threat, and accusation.

3. Check applicable law, platform rules, school or workplace policy.

4. Consider less-restrictive opinions: warnings, labels, filters, takedowns, or moderation.

5. Document the decision, evaluate unintended effects, and protect legitimate expression.


Discussion Case: Techdirt v. Ayyaduari
++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

Ayyadurai sued Techdirt for calling his claim to have invented email fraudulent or bogus.

The case raises the boundary between protected opinion, factual assertion, and defamation. It also illustrates how costly litigation can pressure independent media even before a final ruling.


.. admonition:: Discussion

    Should public claims about technology history receive strong protection for open debate?


Discussion Case: China's Great Firewall
+++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

China's censorship system restricts access to selected websites and topics while maintaining global connectivity. VPNs may help users circumvent restrictions, but access can be blocked by authorities.

The case illustrated the conflict between national control, security, economic connectivity, and free expression.


.. admonition:: Discussion

    What Internet-governance model could protect public safety without suppressing legitimate criticism?
